Battle Quick Start
Citation: §12.1–12.6 · pp. 92–93
The essentials to reference at the table

BEFORE ROUND 1
- Choose a Mission and Deployment Card; set Mission Markers and terrain as specified.
- Roll off; the winner assigns the First Player Marker for Round 1.

FOUR PHASES
- **1 Movement:** Deploy, Move, Disengage, or Hold.
- **2 Assault:** Ranged Attack, Charge, Run, or Hold.
- **3 Combat:** Engaged Ground Units fight using Close Combat attacks.
- **4 Scoring & Cleanup:** Determine control, score VP, check game end, resolve end-of-round effects, refresh, then determine initiative.

CRITICAL DISTINCTIONS
- The first player to **Pass** takes the First Player Marker for the next Phase; passing ends their remaining activations for that Phase.
- An Active Ability generally occurs immediately before declaring an action or after resolving one, not midway through it.
- Always use the full numbered rule and unit/card wording for disputed edge cases.

Activations & Initiative
Citation: §8.1–8.2.2 · pp. 54–55; §8.9.6 · p. 72
Who acts, when to Pass, and who starts next

ALTERNATING ACTIVATIONS
- Each Round has four Phases; Phases 1–3 use alternating Unit activations.
- The First Player Marker holder chooses which player activates first for each Phase.
- Each activated Unit takes one legal action for that Phase.

PASSING
- A player can Pass instead of activating; they must Pass if they have no eligible Units.
- First to Pass claims the First Player Marker for the **next Phase**.
- After Passing, that player makes no further activations in the current Phase, even if the opponent continues.

NEXT ROUND
- During Scoring & Cleanup, the player with fewer VP gains the First Player Marker for the next Round; if tied, roll off.
- A game normally lasts up to five Rounds, subject to the Mission Card.

Core Rules & Unit Cards
Citation: §2.4–2.7 · pp. 29–30; §5.1 · pp. 38–39
Rule priority, keywords, targets, and characteristics

PRIORITY
- Specific Unit, Mission, and ability rules take precedence when they contradict general Core Rules.
- Identical keywords do not stack; where numeric values differ, use the highest value.

COMBAT & FACTION TAGS
- Combat Tags affect target eligibility, weapon Surge interactions, and other abilities.
- Faction Tags determine whether Units and Tactical Cards may join the selected Faction Card.

READING A UNIT
- Speed applies to the Leading Model's movement. Armour and Evade are separate defenses; Evade requires eligibility.
- Supply varies with models remaining. Weapon profiles list Phase, Range, Target, Rate of Attack, Hit, Surge, Damage, and keywords.
- The Ground Combat Tag is distinct from GROUND LEVEL elevation.

Supply & Reserves
Citation: §6.1–6.2 · p. 44; §8.3 · p. 55
Deployment cap and changing Current Supply

CALCULATE
- **Available Supply = Current Supply Pool − combined Current Supply of Friendly Units already on the battlefield.**
- Recalculate a Unit's Current Supply as soon as casualties cross a Supply Profile threshold.
- A Unit can deploy only when its Current Supply is no greater than Available Supply.

ESCALATION
- The Mission Card specifies the Supply Pool and escalation each Round.
- In the final Round (as defined by the Mission Card), Supply Pool restrictions are removed; deployment actions and other restrictions still apply.
- Destroying or weakening Units can release Available Supply for later deployment.

OTHER USES
- Current Supply also determines Mission Marker contests and the Tactical Mass exception to Disengage.
- A Supply 0 Unit can control an uncontested Marker if it meets the normal eligibility requirements.

Coherency & Movement Geometry
Citation: §4.1–4.6 · pp. 33–37; §8.5.3 · pp. 56–58
Leading Model, placement, and links

MOVING
- Choose the Leading Model; measure its movement up to Speed, then set the remaining models.
- At the end of a repositioning action, place models Wholly Within 3 inches of the Leading Model and maintain legal Coherency Links where possible.
- Coherency Links cannot pass through obstacles the Leading Model could not cross; see §4.4 for exceptions.

ILLEGAL PLACEMENT
- If no legal position with a Coherency Link exists for a model, remove that model as a casualty.
- When a position within 3 inches is impossible but a legal link exists, §4.4 describes placing models as close as possible; the Unit may end Out of Coherency.
- Out of Coherency Units cannot contest or control Mission Markers. Casualties alone never force a new Coherency check.

TERRAIN
- Movement, elevation, Access Points, gap clearance, and placement restrictions have additional rules; use the illustrated rules on pp. 33–37 and 56–58 for disputed cases.

Movement Phase
Citation: §8.4–8.5.5 · pp. 56–59
Deploy · Move · Disengage · Hold

CHOOSE ONE ACTION
- **Move:** An Unengaged Unit makes a standard Move.
- **Deploy:** Bring a Unit from Reserves using an Entry Edge, while respecting Available Supply and placement restrictions.
- **Disengage:** An Engaged Unit withdraws using the normal movement procedure.
- **Hold:** Do nothing; the Unit still becomes activated.

DISENGAGE
- Every model must finish outside Engagement Range of all enemies or be removed; if the Leading Model cannot escape, Disengage fails and that model is removed.
- After Disengaging, the Unit cannot Ranged Attack or Charge in the following Assault Phase, unless its Current Supply was greater than the combined Supply of enemies engaged with it.

DEPLOY
- Start the Leading Model in contact with your Entry Edge and move up to Speed; then place the rest in Coherency.
- The deployed Unit may not finish inside the enemy Zone of Influence and must be legally In Coherency.
- An activation marker is set after the action.
=================================================================
Assault Phase
Citation: §8.6–8.7.3 · pp. 59–61
Run · Hold · Ranged Attack · Charge

CHOOSE ONE ACTION
- Only Units activated in Movement on the battlefield act in Assault; Reserves cannot act.
- **Run:** An Unengaged Unit repositions by a standard Move.
- **Hold:** The Unit takes no action.
- **Ranged Attack:** Resolve legal weapon attacks against an eligible target.
- **Charge:** An Unengaged Ground Unit attempts to engage enemy Ground Units.

TARGETING
- Check attacker/target Engagement status, LoS, weapon Range and Target Tags, and other exceptions.
- An Engaged attacker can only target enemy Units with which it is Engaged. Unengaged ranged attackers normally cannot shoot into an Engagement.
- A weapon profile's individual batches are resolved completely before starting the next.

Ranged Attack Resolution
Citation: §8.7.3–8.7.5 · pp. 60–64; §12.4 · p. 92
Check target, build pools, resolve defenses

BEFORE DICE
- Choose a legal enemy target, check Range, LoS and Target Tags; observe any Engagement restrictions.
- Group attacks using the same weapon profile against the same target into one Batch, resolving each Batch completely.

SEQUENCE
- **1:** Build the Attack Pool from eligible models and Rate of Attack; add applicable modifiers and Surge Dice.
- **2:** Roll to Hit. Successful dice move into the Armour Pool.
- **3:** Resolve Surge against a matching Combat Tag; its effect may bypass Armour as specified.
- **4:** Defender rolls Armour against remaining dice, then Evade if eligible.
- **5:** Apply weapon Damage to remaining dice and remove casualties using the correct allocation rules.

IMPORTANT
- Not every defender gets an Evade Roll. Check the specific entitlement and a non-null Evade characteristic.
- Template weapons, multiple attack batches, and engaged-model casualty removal have additional procedures in §8.7.

Charge & Engagement
Citation: §7.2 · p. 53; §8.7.7 · pp. 65–66
Reach the target without confusing Charge with melee

DECLARE
- Charge targets must be enemy Ground Units; Line of Sight is not required.
- Roll D6 for Charge Distance. The Leading Model can move up to Speed plus the roll, subject to Charge rules.

RESOLVE
- The Leading Model must be able to end within the 1-inch Engagement Range of all declared targets for the Charge to succeed.
- After success, place the rest of the Unit using the Charge placement priority: Base-to-Base, Engagement Range, then Coherency.
- If the Charge fails, the Unit stays in place and its activation ends.

TIMING
- The Charge takes place in the **Assault Phase**; normal Close Combat attacks are made in the later **Combat Phase**.

Close Combat
Citation: §8.8–8.8.1 · pp. 67–68; §12.5 · p. 93
Which models fight and how to resolve attacks

ACTIVATION
- All Engaged Ground Units must activate in Combat; players alternate.
- A player only Passes once no eligible Engaged Units remain. Flying Units are never Engaged.

CLOSE COMBAT ATTACK
- Optionally **Close Ranks**: Leading Model moves up to 3 inches and ends closer to an already Engaged enemy; follow the repositioning rules.
- **Fighting Rank:** models within 1 inch of an enemy. **Supporting Rank:** models Base-to-Base with a friendly Fighting Rank model.
- Eligible models attack with Combat Phase weapons; resolve attacks using the attack sequence and applicable melee exceptions.
- Evade during Combat requires a specific granting rule or keyword.

DISPUTES
- Use §8.8.1 to resolve multiple Engagements and the priority for removing models from Engaged Units.

Line of Sight & Cover
Citation: §7.1–7.2.1 · pp. 45–53
Top-down visibility, elevation, and terrain

LOS BASICS
- StarCraft TMG uses top-down (2D) Line of Sight, from the acting model's base to the target model's base.
- Terrain's Effective Size and elevation affect whether the line is blocked or grants Cover.
- A target or attack can be illegal even though the models look visible from a miniature's eye level.

CHECK BEFORE ROLLING
- Determine whether models are individually eligible, then check their weapon Range and the target's Combat Tag.
- Full Cover, Direct Cover, High Ground Cover, and Flying interactions are governed by distinct illustrated rules.
- Because this section is diagram-heavy, consult the original PDF pp. 45–52 for exact terrain/visibility cases.

Scoring & Cleanup
Citation: §8.9–8.10 · pp. 69–72; §12.6 · p. 93
Mission Marker control and next-round initiative

WHO CAN CONTEST?
- A Unit must be on the battlefield, In Coherency, and have at least one model within 3 inches with LoS to the Marker on the same elevation.
- Flying Units cannot contest or control Mission Markers.
- Sum **Current Supply**, not model counts, for eligible contesting Units.

CONTROL
- Higher summed Supply wins. A tie is Contested; it does not transfer an existing controller's ownership.
- **Sticky control:** a controlled Marker stays owned after friendly Units leave until the opponent takes it with a higher contesting Supply total.
- Scoring Victory Points follows each Mission Card, not a universal fixed VP value.

CLEANUP
- Check end-of-game conditions, then end-of-round effects.
- Remove ordinary markers/tokens; keep STAY IN PLAY, Damage Markers, Mission Markers, and Faction Indicators representing control.
- Refresh Exhausted Faction and Tactical Cards. Player with fewer VP gets next Round's First Player Marker; ties require a roll-off.

Abilities, Reactions & Cards
Citation: §2.7 · pp. 29–30; §10.1–10.5.2 · pp. 79–81
Timing windows, resource payments, and frequency

ACTIVE / PASSIVE / REACTION
- **Active:** use only when the Unit is activated, immediately before declaring its action or after it fully resolves; not during the action.
- **Passive:** continuously applies when conditions are met; no activation is needed.
- **Reaction:** declare at the exact triggering event; missed timing cannot be used retroactively.

LIMITS
- A named Active or Reaction ability is generally once per Round per Unit, unless REPEATABLE or another rule says otherwise.
- Each player may resolve **one Reaction per Activation**. When both respond to the same trigger, the Active Player resolves theirs first.
- Reserves Units normally cannot use Active, Passive, or Reaction abilities unless a rule specifically allows it.

RESOURCES
- Terran uses **CP**; Zerg uses **BM**; Protoss uses **PE** in this PDF.
- Exhaust Ready faction/tactical cards to pay full costs. Unused generated resource is lost; it cannot be banked for another ability.
- An Exhausted card provides no resources or abilities until refreshed in Cleanup.

Army Building
Citation: §9.1–9.3 · pp. 73–78; §5.2–5.6 · pp. 39–43
Tags, slots, upgrades, missions and deployment

FACTION & COSTS
- Pick exactly one Faction Card. Every Faction Tag on a selected Unit or Tactical Card must appear on that Faction Card.
- Use Minerals for Units and upgrades; Vespene Gas purchases Tactical Cards that add Army Slots.
- Required slot type and count depend on each Unit's starting Supply and profile.

UPGRADES
- A normal purchased upgrade applies to the whole Unit; a SPECIALIST upgrade goes on a single nominated model.
- Replacements remove the specified original weapon. Different SPECIALIST upgrades may be assigned to different models.
- Resolve each differing weapon profile as a separate attack Batch.

PREPARING
- Follow the Mission / Deployment draft and Battlefield Setup rules; reference the Deployment Card for Entry Edges and Marker positions.
- Use the official October 9, 2026 balance table for current points; the printed Core Rules appendix contains earlier values.
